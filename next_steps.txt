1. Handling "Unknown" Columns (positin1)
Validation: You are correct to be suspicious of positin1. However, looking at the dataset documentation, positin1 is actually a valid column (likely a typo in the original dataset for "position at start"). Data documentation says: "positin1: station number where started".
Logic: treating it as "Unknown" is safe if you don't use it, but be aware it does have a meaning.
2. The "100 vs 10" Scale Issue
Critical: You correctly identified that attr1_1 through amb1_1 (and similar waves) use different scales (100-point allocation vs 1-10 rating).
Flaw in current logic: If you impute the mean before fixing this, you will corrupt the data. The mean of a 100-point scale column will be massively higher than the 1-10 scale values.
Fix: You must normalize these columns to a common scale (e.g., convert everything to percentage or a 1-10 scale) before calculating the mean or imputing values.
3. Imputing age_o and race_o
Great intuition: Your idea to "get the real age in other rows" is excellent and the best approach.
Why: Every partner (pid) is also a participant (iid) in another row. You can create a look-up table (dictionary) mapping iid -> age and iid -> race from the dataset itself. Use this to fill in missing age_o and race_o based on pid instead of using a generic mean.
4. fillna("Unknown") for Object Columns
Risk: be careful with columns like income. In the dataframe preview, income looks like "69,487.00". Pandas sees this as an "object" (string) because of the comma.
Improvement: If you fill "Unknown" now, you can't convert it to a number later. You should clean these specific columns (remove commas, convert to float) first, and then use numeric imputation (like median) instead of "Unknown".
5. fillna(int(mean)) for All Other Columns
Risk 1 (Categorical Codes): Columns like field_cd, career_c, race, and date are "categorical" but encoded as numbers.
Example: race 1=Black, 2=White, 3=Latino. If you have a missing race and impute the mean (e.g., 2.4), and cast to integer (2), you are forcing everyone to be "White" just because of the math.
Better approach: Use the Mode (most frequent value) or a distinct "Unknown" code (like -1) for categorical codes.
Risk 2 (Integer Casting): Casting the mean to int throws away precision for continuous variables (like ratings attr_o). A rating of 7.5 is meaningful; rounding it to 7 loses signal.
